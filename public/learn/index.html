The Valley Tote case study

The website was the easy part.
Getting a customer was harder.

We used AI to turn a moving-tote business concept into a website. The project developed into a public site with a mascot, inquiry forms and payment infrastructure. The initial build was fast. The early test gave us a separate question to answer: would interested visitors take the next step?

The 6m 43s interval covers the first private prototype only. Prior research, public launch, payment setup and later revisions are excluded. Meta figures cover a partial November 6–8, 2026 reporting window, not all advertising or project costs. Impressions are ad displays, not unique people; link clicks are not customers.

What the early results showed: a working prototype and measurable paid-ad attention. The snapshot at noon Eastern on November 8 showed zero genuine site requests and zero verified paid deposits. The rental concept did not become an operating business. These figures do not establish customer demand or profitability.

Know what the numbers mean

Learn the process, with the context intact.

Will my project be finished in seven minutes?

No completion-time promise is being made. This timer describes one first prototype after earlier research. Your scope, starting materials, integrations, revisions and review requirements will affect the work.

Did the ads produce paying customers?

The reported snapshot showed 28 link clicks and zero genuine site requests or verified paid deposits as of noon Eastern on November 8. Paid-ad attention was measurable; sales and validated demand were not established.

Is $9.36 the cost of building the site?

No. It is the spend reported in a partial Meta advertising snapshot. It excludes other advertising, software, platform, payment-processing and time costs. It is not a complete project budget.
